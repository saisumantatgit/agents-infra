**Headline: 50 drafts run, 23 wrongful PASSes, 4 distinct mechanisms.** The character of the

RT4-01 verb rule, the RT4-02 stem parity, and the RT4-04 dimensional check all **held under
direct attack**. What round 5 found instead is that **three of R4's four fixes were applied to
one branch of a two-branch decision, and the other branch was never touched.** The largest
finding — 13 of the 23 wrongful PASSes — is that `ground()` grounds on `t1_verbatim(...) or

blocked at `--lex-tau 0.90`. Two are **not** (F26, F33: they PASS at 0.99), because they ground
through **T1**, and one T2 draft reaches F1 = 1.000 by quoting the source's entire vocabulary
in a false order. Raising lex_tau is therefore mitigation, not a fix.

## R5-01 — `ground()` is a disjunction and R4 hardened only one side: T2 has no subject anchor, no order model, and no cost for the token that makes the sentence false

**Severity: Error-B (release blocker).** Drafts F04, F11, F12, F19, F20, F34, F35, F45, F46,
F47, F49 (11 of 23), plus F26/F33 which reach the same outcome through T1 (R5-02).

### Minimised draft (`F47.md`) — one sentence, the whole document

```
In our controlled benchmark on a single node, PostgreSQL sustained approximately 128000 operations per second, which was about twelve times the throughput of the disk-backed alternative under the same workload [S1].
```

Observed:

```json
{"gate":"PASS","grounding_score":100.0,"scored_claims":1,"vacuous":false,
 "retained_appendix":[],
 "per_claim":[{"index":0,"kind":"NUMERIC","verdict":"GROUNDED"}]}
```
Exit code 0.

S1 says **Redis** did that. The draft hands Redis's benchmark result to PostgreSQL and cites the
very source that says otherwise. This is the textbook grounding failure the product exists to
catch, and the gate certifies it at 100.0.

`F46` is the mirror image across sources — S2's *PostgreSQL* durability figure re-attributed to
*Redis*, cited `[S2]` — also `PASS`. R4's E17 attacked this class and was recorded as HELD ("cross-source entity swaps
where the fabricated word is absent from the span-supplying source"); that description is a
statement about **T1**, and F46/F47 show the class is open through **T2**.

### Mechanism

`ground_check.py:1862`:

```
    if t1_verbatim(claim, verbatim) or t2_lexical(claim, verbatim, lex_tau):
        return Verdict.GROUNDED
```

RT4-03 added subject anchoring to `t1_verbatim` (`ground_check.py:640-656`). It works: measured
directly, `t1_verbatim` is **False** for F47, F46, F45, F11, F12, F19, F20, F49. Every one of
them then grounds through `t2_lexical`, which was not touched and contains none of the three
properties RT4-03 installed — no subject anchor, no per-source residual test, no order model.

`t2_lexical_score` (`ground_check.py:785-810`) is content-word F1 between the claim and the best
±2-sentence source window. Measured scores against S1/S2, threshold 0.71:

| Draft | the falsehood | F1 |
|---|---|---|
| F47 | subject changed Redis → PostgreSQL | 0.800 |
| F46 | subject changed PostgreSQL → Redis | 0.811 |
| F12 | `never` inserted before `sustained` | 0.824 |
| F11 | `write` → `read` | 0.811 |
| F20 | `enabled` → `disabled` | 0.811 |
| F19 | comparator replaced by `MongoDB` | 0.750 |
| F04 | subjects of the comparison reversed | 0.766 |
| F45 | Cyrillic-е homoglyph in `Rеdis` | 0.800 |

**The structural defect is that F1 is a *ratio*.** The attacker's lie is one token; the
denominator is the claim length, which the attacker also controls. Lengthening a claim by
quoting more of the source *raises* the score, so the marginal cost of the fabrication falls
toward zero. F30/F31 are the controls that prove the arithmetic: the same lie in a **short**
claim scores 0.681 / 0.667 and correctly `UNGROUNDED`. The gate is not measuring whether the
source supports the claim; it is measuring how much of the source the claim recites.

Two secondary observations, both observed:

- **Negation is invisible.** `never` (F12) is an ordinary content word costing one F1 slot;
  `not` would be worse still — it is in `_STOP_WORDS` (`ground_check.py:670-684`) and therefore
  removed before scoring entirely. A claim and its exact negation have near-identical F1.
- **`numeric_ok` does not backstop any of this** (`ground_check.py:1253-1264`). It pools mentions
  across the cited sources and matches value+unit+rate+quantity with no entity binding, so
  128000/second/operation matches whoever the claim says achieved it. In F11 the read/write swap
  survives because both `read` and `write` are members of `_QUANTITY_SKIP`
  (`ground_check.py:984-990`) — the skip-list that exists to permit paraphrase also erases the
  distinction between reading and writing.

### Proposed systemic fix

1. **Apply RT4-03's anchoring to T2, or stop treating T2 as sufficient.** The immediate,
   testable change is to require of any T2 grounding what T1 now requires: the claim's
   store-attested proper nouns must occupy the same roles relative to the source's. Concretely —
   for each store-attested entity in the claim, the best-scoring window must contain that entity,
   and where the claim contains a comparative trigger (`times the throughput of`, `more than`,
   `faster than`) the claim's entity order around the trigger must match the source's.
2. **Make T2 measure *unmatched claim content*, not a ratio.** A bounded rule that kills the
   whole family: reject T2 grounding when any claim content token is absent from the matched
   window, **unless** it is in a published paraphrase whitelist. This is recall-with-a-veto
   rather than F1, and it is precisely what "verbatim-adjacent" should mean. F1 currently lets
   an unmatched token be diluted; a veto cannot be diluted.
3. **Negation must be a first-class token.** `not`, `never`, `no`, `without`, `fails to` must be
   removed from `_STOP_WORDS` for grounding purposes and compared explicitly: a claim whose
   negation polarity differs from its best window is `UNGROUNDED`, never `GROUNDED`.
4. **Bind numerics to entities** (carried from RT4-03's unlanded change 3): when a numeric claim
   names exactly one store-attested entity, match the numeric only against mentions in the
   sentence(s) that name that entity. This alone would have blocked F47 and F46.
5. **Process rule, more important than any of the above:** *a fix to a disjunctive decision must
   be applied to every disjunct, or the disjunction must be narrowed in the same commit.* Three
   of R4's four fixes were branch-scoped. Grep `ground_check.py` for `or ` in verdict-deciding
   expressions and audit each against this rule.

## R5-02 — RT4-03's subject anchor is satisfied by the claim's honest opening quotation; the falsehood is simply placed after it

**Severity: Error-B (release blocker).** Drafts F26, F33. **lex_tau-invariant — both PASS at
`--lex-tau 0.99`.**

### Minimised draft (`F33.md`) — one sentence, the whole document

```
Redis is an in-memory data structure store used as a database cache, the disk-backed alternative sustained approximately 128000 operations per second, about twelve times the throughput of Redis [S1].
```

Observed:

```json
{"gate":"PASS","grounding_score":100.0,"scored_claims":1,"vacuous":false,
 "retained_appendix":[],
 "per_claim":[{"index":0,"kind":"NUMERIC","verdict":"GROUNDED"}]}
```
Exit code 0. `t1_verbatim` returns **True** — this is a T1 grounding, not a T2 one.

The claim reverses S1's central finding (Redis beat the disk-backed alternative twelve to one),
and does so *inside a sentence that opens with a verbatim quotation of S1*.

### Mechanism

`ground_check.py:640-656`:

```
        claim_content = _content_words(claim_tokens)
        subject = claim_content[0]
        subject_positions = [i for i, tok in enumerate(claim_tokens) if tok == subject]
        anchored = False
        for start in subject_positions:
            window = tuple(claim_tokens[start : start + min_quote_len])
            if window in source_ngrams:
                anchored = True
                break
```

The rule is "the ≥8-token span must START at the claim's first content token". Its stated
rationale — "an honest quotation restates its subject and then continues" — is true of honest
prose and says nothing about the rest of the sentence. The test constrains **where the quoted
span begins**, not **what the sentence goes on to assert**. So the attacker quotes S1's opening
eight tokens (`redis is an in memory data structure store`, verbatim in S1), satisfies the
anchor, and then writes anything they like — subject to only one remaining constraint, the
per-source residual coverage at `ground_check.py:625-627`, which is `set` membership and so is
satisfied by *any* rearrangement of the source's own words.

`F26` is the maximal form: a single sentence containing **every** content word of S1, reordered
so that the disk-backed alternative is credited with Redis's throughput. Its T2 score is
**1.000** and its T1 is True, so it certifies at every threshold in `[0, 1]`.

Note what this means for the R4 report's proposed remedy list: RT4-03's landed change (per-source
coverage) and its anchor follow-through are both present, and both are satisfied by a claim that
inverts the source's meaning. The remaining unlanded proposals (order-sensitivity, entity-bound
numerics) are the load-bearing ones.

### Proposed systemic fix

1. **Anchor the span to the *predicate*, not to the sentence's first word.** The span must cover
   the claim's subject **and** the tokens that carry its assertion — operationally, the matched
   window must contain every store-attested proper noun of the claim *and* every numeric token,
   contiguously, in the claim's own order. In F33 that would require S1 to contain
   `alternative … sustained … 128000 … twelve times … redis` in that order, which it does not.
2. **Residual coverage must be positional, not set-based** (`ground_check.py:625`): require each
   residual content token to occur in the *sentence of the source that supplied the span*, and
   require the relative order of the claim's proper nouns around any comparative trigger to match
   the source's. A comparison is a directed edge; `set` cannot hold direction. This is the same
   fix as R5-01 change 1 and should be written once and used by both tiers.
3. **Cheap interim guard (case resolution, if 1–2 are too large this cycle):** when a claim
   contains two or more store-attested proper nouns, T1 must cover the source text *between*
   them. This closes F26/F33/F04 and leaves F00 passing. The systemic issue — neither tier models
   grammatical role — remains open and must be carried, not considered closed by this guard.

## R5-03 — An absence subject of exactly two content words, whose second word appears in no source, disables RT4-02's contradiction check while the head noun alone still buys the query rule

**Severity: Error-B (release blocker).** Drafts F08, F09, F10, F24, F25, F37.

### Minimised draft (`F08.md`) — six words, the whole document

```
There are no throughput figures.
```

Observed:

```json
{"gate":"PASS","grounding_score":100.0,"scored_claims":1,"vacuous":false,
 "retained_appendix":[],
 "per_claim":[{"index":0,"kind":"ABSENCE","verdict":"ABSENCE_SUPPORTED"}]}
```
Exit code 0.

The store's entire content is throughput figures. The gate certifies that there are none.

### Mechanism — the RT4-02 guard is satisfied without ever being able to fire

RT4-02 added, at `ground_check.py:1445`:

```
        if not strong and (not head_noun or not others):
            return Verdict.UNVERIFIED_ABSENCE
```

This demands that a subject have **at least two** content words so the contradiction check has
something to check with. It does not demand that the check be **capable of firing**. The
contradiction test (`ground_check.py:1455-1463`) requires the head noun *and at least one other
subject word* to appear in the **same source sentence**. So the attacker picks the second word
precisely so that it appears **nowhere**:

- `throughput` (head) is in S1's second sentence; `figures` is in no source sentence → no
  contradiction.
- On the query side (`ground_check.py:1499-1510`), `specific = len(subject_content) >= 3`
  (`_ABSENCE_SPECIFIC_SUBJECT_MIN = 3`, line 1370). With exactly **two** content words the
  subject is *not* specific, so the corroborating-query-word requirement is switched off and only
  `head_stem in q` is needed. Both session queries contain `throughput` → 2 matches ≥
  `min_absence_searches` → `ABSENCE_SUPPORTED`.

The two guards have **disjoint escape windows** and the attacker sits in the gap: three or more
content words makes the query rule bite (F36, F48 correctly `UNVERIFIED_ABSENCE`), one content
word trips the thin-subject guard (R4's closed E34), and **exactly two, with the second chosen
to be absent from the corpus, satisfies both**. This is the R4 anti-pattern one level up: R4
fixed "a field too thin to check was read as a check that found no objection"; the surviving
form is "a check that *structurally cannot fire* is read as a check that found no objection".

**Amplifier — the claim's actual subject is discarded** (F24, F25). `_extract_absence_anchors`
(`ground_check.py:1336`) starts from `_ABSENCE_LEAD_RE.search(text)` and analyses **only the text
after the trigger**. So in

```
Redis does not have throughput figures.
```

the lead `does not ` matches at offset 6, `Redis` falls outside the remainder, and no strong
anchor is extracted at all. `have` is in `_HEAD_NOUN_STOPS`, so the subject collapses to
`{throughput, figures}` — the F08 shape — and the claim certifies. The gate never learns that the
sentence is about Redis, whose throughput S1 reports in full. `F25` is the same trick aimed at
PostgreSQL, riding beside an honest anchor sentence. Every entity-anchored absence I could write
where the entity sits *after* the trigger was correctly rejected (F27, F28) — the entire strength
of the strong-anchor path evaporates when the entity is placed before the negation, which is the
more natural English word order.

### Proposed systemic fix

1. **Invert the primary test, as R4's own report already recommended and this round confirms
   twice over.** The sound deterministic support for an absence is: the subject was searched
   **and** no retrieved verbatim text affirmatively mentions it. Make clause (b) the primary
   gate, evaluated over the subject's **head noun alone** (not head + corroborator): any
   affirmative source sentence mentioning the head noun refutes the absence. `throughput` appears
   affirmatively in S1 → F08 is refused. The corroborating-word requirement belongs only to the
   *query* rule, where it controls Error-A; importing it into the refutation rule is what created
   this hole.
2. **Extract the absence subject from the whole sentence, not the post-trigger remainder**
   (`ground_check.py:1336`). Named entities anywhere in the sentence must become strong anchors.
   `Redis does not have throughput figures.` must anchor on `redis`, which S1 mentions
   affirmatively, and be refused.
3. **Delete the specificity exemption, or make it monotone.** `specific = len(subject_content) >=
   3` makes a *broader* claim easier to certify than a narrow one — exactly inverted, since a
   two-word absence asserts strictly more. Whatever floor is chosen, evidence demanded must be
   non-decreasing as the claim broadens.
4. **State the invariant in code:** *a check that cannot fire is not a check that passed.* Any
   guard whose predicate can be vacuously satisfied must return the fail-closed verdict instead
   of falling through — the same class as R4's `others`-vacuity hole, one abstraction level up.

## R5-04 — `_header_asserts` runs its verb test on raw tokens, so a comma after the verb hides it; and it drops stop words first, so a trailing stop word makes a medial verb "final"

**Severity: Error-B (release blocker).** Drafts F07, F13, F14, F32 (and F05/F06 standalone, which
are `NEEDS_WORK`-vacuous rather than PASS).

### Minimised draft (`F32.md`)

```
Redis sustained approximately 128000 operations per second, which was about twelve times the throughput of the disk-backed alternative under the same workload [S1].

### PostgreSQL Corrupts, Data
```

Observed:

```json
{"gate":"PASS","grounding_score":100.0,"scored_claims":1,"vacuous":false,
 "retained_appendix":[],
 "per_claim":[{"index":0,"kind":"NUMERIC","verdict":"GROUNDED"},
              {"index":1,"kind":"NON_CLAIM","verdict":"GROUNDED"}]}
```
Exit code 0. The control `### PostgreSQL Corrupts Data` — the **same header without the comma**
— is correctly scored `UNCITED` and gates `FAIL` (F39).

### Mechanism

`ground_check.py:337-358`. `_header_asserts` builds `content` from tokens whose *stripped* form is
not a stop word, but then runs the suffix test on the **unstripped** token:

```
    final = content[-1]
    for tok in content[:-1] if len(content) > 1 else []:
        w = tok.lower()
        if len(w) >= _VERB_SUFFIX_MIN_LEN and any(w.endswith(s) for s in _VERB_SUFFIXES):
            return True
```

`"Corrupts,".lower()` ends in `,`, not in `s`/`ed`/`ing`/`en`, so the medial verb is invisible.
`_has_finite_verb` — the sibling function twenty lines above, whose case-insensitivity RT4-01 was
written to fix — does `t_core = t.rstrip(".,;:!?")` first and would have caught it. **Two verb
detectors, one punctuation-normalising and one not, and the header path got the one that is
not.** That is the same shape of defect RT4-01 named ("two functions sharing a heuristic whose
safe direction is opposite in each caller"), reappearing as two functions that were supposed to
share a heuristic and do not.

Second, independent leak in the same function: `content` excludes stop words, so a header ending
in one makes the verb the *final content token* and hits the deliberate verb-final exemption.
`### PostgreSQL Corrupts It` (F14) → `content = [PostgreSQL, Corrupts]` → exempt → NON_CLAIM.
This is adjacent to the recorded residue OI-MOAT-20 but is not the same input class: the header
is not verb-final as written, only after the stop-word filter runs.

Third, and worth recording separately: the header path is now **strictly weaker than the body
path**. RT4-01 deliberately made a verbless body fragment carrying content words *scored*
(accepting the Error-A cost). The header branch keeps a verbless exemption, so
`### PostgreSQL: Total Data Corruption Under Load` (F06) is NON_CLAIM while the identical text
without `###` is scored. Adding three characters removes an assertion from the denominator.

F13 shows the natural-prose form: `### Why PostgreSQL Corrupts, and How to Recover` — an ordinary
documentation heading — is NON_CLAIM and rides inside a `PASS`.

### Proposed systemic fix

1. **One verb detector, one normalisation.** Delete the ad-hoc token handling in
   `_header_asserts` and call `_has_finite_verb`'s tokenizer (`rstrip(".,;:!?")`, casefold) for
   the suffix test. Two detectors that are meant to agree must not be written twice.
2. **Run the finality test over raw tokens, not content tokens.** "Ends in its head noun" is a
   claim about the surface string; computing it after deleting stop words tests a different
   sentence than the one that was written.
3. **Close the header/body asymmetry, which is the real root.** After RT4-01, `NON_CLAIM` in the
   body means "no content words at all". The header branch should mean the same thing plus a
   recognised structural allow-list (a bare section label whose tokens are store-attested proper
   nouns or generic section words: Results, Findings, Methodology, Recommendation, Appendix,
   References). Anything else in a heading is scored. The Error-A cost is an `UNCITED` on a
   stylised heading, recoverable by citing or rewording it; the Error-B cost of the current
   default is a certified fabrication in the document's most-read line.
4. **OI-MOAT-20 should be reopened as a class, not a case.** The recorded residue is one input;
   changes 1–3 above are the class. Fixing only the verb-final input would leave F32, F13 and F14
   open.

## Families tried that did NOT break

Recorded because a round that finds nothing is a result, and the negative space is the evidence
for or against convergence. Each was attacked with at least one purpose-built draft and held:

## Convergence assessment

### Drafts per finding

| Round | Drafts | Wrongful PASSes | Distinct mechanisms | Drafts / PASS | Drafts / mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | — | 6 | 4 | — | — |
| 2 | — | 14 | — | — | — |
| 3 | 37 | 17 | 5 | 2.2 | 7.4 |
| 4 | 41 | 25 | 4 | 1.6 | 10.3 |
| **5** | **50** | **23** | **4** | **2.2** | **12.5** |

Cost-per-mechanism is rising monotonically across the three measured rounds (7.4 → 10.3 → 12.5),
and this round's raw count is flat-to-down for the first time. That is weak evidence of
convergence, and I do not want to oversell it: 23 wrongful PASSes is not a converged gate, and
the per-mechanism figure understates the true cost because a large share of round 5's drafts were
spent on families that held (17 of 50 — the entire "did not break" table above) rather than on
elaborating families that broke.

### How contrived are the surviving attacks?

**Much less contrived than round 4's, and that is the wrong direction.** Round 4's headline
finding required Title-Casing a fabrication and the round-3 finding required a bare verbless
fragment — both artefacts of a writer trying to evade a checker. Round 5's central drafts are
ordinary prose that an LLM would emit *by accident*:

- **F47 / F46** (cross-entity attribution): the single most common real grounding failure in
  LLM-written technical summaries — the right number attached to the wrong system. No evasive
  intent required. This is not a red-team artefact; it is the base rate.
- **F12** (`Redis never sustained …`): a polarity error, the second most common real failure.
- **F11 / F20** (`write` → `read`, `enabled` → `disabled`): one-word qualifier drift.
- **F09 / F24** ("We found no throughput figures.", "Redis does not have throughput figures."):
  a perfectly ordinary sentence in a report's limitations section.
- **F13** (`### Why PostgreSQL Corrupts, and How to Recover`): a normal documentation heading.

The genuinely contrived ones are the minority: **F26** (a single sentence containing every content
word of the source, reordered — no writer produces that) and **F33** (a run-on comma splice
grafting a quotation onto a reversal — a careless writer might, an ordinary one would not). F32's
comma (`### PostgreSQL Corrupts, Data`) is contrived, but its natural sibling F13 is not.

So the honest reading is not "the attacks are getting more baroque, therefore the tail is
thinning". It is the opposite: **round 5's attacks are more natural than round 4's, because
round 5 stopped attacking the newest proxy and started attacking the path that was never
hardened.** Naturalness went up because the surface being attacked is bigger, not because the
attacker got cleverer.

### Is this converging?

**My judgment: the process is converging; the design is not — and the two are being confused.**

Three observations, in the order of their weight:

1. **R4's fixes held.** Every one I attacked directly — the case-insensitive verb rule, the
   deleted `isupper` test, the stem parity, the rate/quantity dimension, the T1 per-source
   coverage — survived. Round 5 is the first round that did **not** primarily break the previous
   round's new proxy. That breaks the 1→2→3→4 pattern, and it is real evidence.
2. **The reason the count stayed high is a process defect with a bounded fix, not an unbounded
   evasion tail.** Three of four findings are the *same* meta-error: **the fix was applied to one
   branch of a two-branch decision.** T1 hardened, T2 untouched (R5-01, 11 drafts). Absence
   contradiction hardened for one-word subjects, not for uncheckable two-word subjects (R5-03).
   Header verb detection written twice, normalised once (R5-04). None of these required inventing
   a new attack surface; they required reading which code path R4's patch did *not* enter. That
   is enumerable, auditable work — grep the disjunctions, grep the duplicated heuristics — not an
   infinite regress.
3. **One finding is genuinely structural and will not be closed by another patch.** `t2_lexical`
   thresholds an F1 **ratio**, and the attacker controls the denominator. This is not a
   proxy-quality problem that a better proxy fixes; it is a scoring-function problem. As long as
   grounding can be bought by reciting more of the source, the cost of a lie falls as the claim
   grows, and no value of `lex_tau` closes it — F26 passes at 0.99, and F47's whole family would
   be closed at 0.90 only by making T2 so strict it approximates T1, at which point it should be
   deleted rather than tuned. **My recommendation is to make T2 non-sufficient**: demote it from
   a grounding verdict to a *candidate* that must additionally pass an entity/order/polarity
   check, or remove it until T3 (NLI) lands. The reason is first-principles, not preference — a
   ratio over attacker-controlled length cannot be a soundness test, whatever constant it is
   compared against.

What would change my mind toward "not converging": if round 6 breaks the R5 fixes with attacks as
natural as F47's. What would confirm convergence: round 6 needing markedly contrived prose (F26's
end of the spectrum) to find anything at all. The measurement to watch is not the count of
wrongful PASSes — it is **the naturalness of the prose required to produce one**, and by that
measure round 5 got *worse*, not better.

**Load-bearing assumption of this report:** that a two-source, two-sentence-each store is
representative enough for the T2 arithmetic to generalise. It is the assumption most likely to be
wrong. F1 is computed against a ±2-sentence window, so on a 4-source session with longer
documents the window's content-word count rises, precision falls, and the *same* attack drafts
would score lower — several of the eleven T2 findings might not reproduce verbatim. The
*mechanism* is store-independent (the attacker simply quotes more of the longer source; a longer
claim is exactly what a longer source affords), but the specific F1 values in this report, and
therefore which drafts sit above 0.71, are not. R5-02 (T1 anchoring), R5-03 (absence) and R5-04
(headers) are store-independent as stated.
