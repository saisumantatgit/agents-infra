# J-15 / OI-DEC-02 — should rhetorical questions be scored?

**One-word ruling wanted. My recommendation: NO EXEMPTION — fix the report instead.**

## The measurement that settles it

Ten cpc-book manuscript chapters, **1,337 scored claims**, counted tonight with
`decompose` + `classify` as deployed:

| | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| scored claims | 1,337 | — |
| question-form among them | **106** | **7.9%** |
| question-form **carrying a citation** | **0** | **0.0%** |

Range across chapters 5.2%–10.7%. (Method: a claim whose text ends in `?`.
Earlier sessions quoted 9.4% on a 1,121-claim set; this is a different chapter
selection and my own detector, so treat 7.9% as this measurement's number and
not as a correction of that one.)

**Every single question-form claim in 1,337 is UNCITED, not UNGROUNDED.** They
are not failing to ground. They are failing to carry a citation, because they
are questions and nobody cites a question.

Real examples: `Which court listens?` · `Maintainable?` · `Prospects?` ·
`Is the debt gone?` · `## QUESTION 4 — So what can they take?`

## Why this changes the shape of the decision

The worry on record was the smuggling case —
`Isn't Redis capable of 128000 operations per second [S1]?` classifies NUMERIC
and is scored today. **That form occurs zero times in 1,337 real-prose claims.**
The thing the exemption would protect against is not what the corpus contains,
and the thing the corpus contains is not what the exemption was debated over.

## The frameworks, and where they land

**First principles.** The gate's job is: *no fabrication is certified.* An
uncited question asserts nothing the gate can check, and today it reads UNCITED
— which blocks PASS. That is already the correct fail-closed answer. The problem
is not the verdict. The problem is that the author cannot tell a question that
needs a citation from a sentence that may be fabricated, because both arrive in
one undifferentiated list.

**Inversion.** Exempting moves 106 claims OUT of the denominator. `Isn't Redis
capable of 128000 operations per second?` — no citation — would become
NON_CLAIM, and a reader reads that sentence as an assertion. Error-A falls,
Error-B rises. Unrecoverable traded for recoverable: the invariant forbids it.

**Via negativa.** The thing to remove is not the claims. It is the CONFLATION in
the report.

**Goodhart.** "Percent of a page flagged" is the metric under pressure, and
exempting questions is the cheapest way to improve it without improving
anything. That is the definition of the failure.

**Occam / MVP.** Group question-form UNCITED claims under their own heading in
the report. No verdict changes, no taxonomy change, no ADR, nothing enters or
leaves the denominator. Roughly an afternoon.

**Chesterton's Fence.** Questions are scored because round 3 and round 4 both
died on rules that let text escape the denominator on a surface property. This
fence was built by those rounds and it is load-bearing.

**Second-order.** Round 7 found OI-MOAT-30 and OI-MOAT-31 tonight — an
unpunctuated header welding the line below into its exemption, and
`_header_asserts` keyed on whether a noun ends in "s". Both are claims escaping
the denominator. Adding a third exemption class while two are open and untriaged
is the wrong week for it.

**Regret minimisation.** If I exempt and it smuggles one fabrication into a
client deliverable, that is the product's whole thesis broken. If I do not
exempt, an author sees twelve questions listed as "needs a citation". The
asymmetry is not close.

## What this does and does not buy

**Does:** the author reads *"12 questions carry no citation"* separately from
*"3 claims may be unsupported"*, and the second list is the one that matters.
Trust in the tool survives contact with a real page.

**Does not:** move Demo criterion D-2 #1. The 7.9% is still flagged, correctly.
Anyone claiming this fixes the false-alarm rate is quoting the wrong number —
**it makes the 7.9% interpretable, not smaller.** The honest path to D-2 #1 is
still Error-A, and Error-A still rests on labels whose reproducibility failed.

## Load-bearing assumption

That cpc-book prose is representative of what the gate will see. It is legal
explainer writing, unusually question-heavy by genre; a technical report would
likely sit lower. If the intended user writes something question-sparse, the
7.9% overstates the problem and the case for doing nothing at all gets stronger,
never weaker. **No sample makes the exemption the right call.**
