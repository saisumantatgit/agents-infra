## R27-01 — A universal negative claim grounds on a source sentence that restricts its scope | HIGH · REGRESSION
Mechanism: B1 moves "X has no Y" / "X no longer Y" / "X was not Y" from ABSENCE (always refused) to FACTUAL. T1 then accepts the claim as a contiguous PREFIX of a longer source sentence. The polarity guard compares (negator, next word) pairs, and those match, so the trailing scope qualifier ("in mice", "under 40", "at night", "until 2019") that limits or reverses the negation is never read.
Reproduction (claim / source sentence → HEAD | base):
- sidefx: "Drug X has no known side effects." / "Drug X has no known side effects in mice; in humans it causes nausea." → PASS GROUNDED | FAIL UNVERIFIED_ABSENCE
- scope: "The vaccine caused no deaths among trial participants." / "...among trial participants under 40, while three older participants died." → PASS | FAIL UNVERIFIED_ABSENCE
- nolonger: "The plant no longer emits sulfur." / "...no longer emits sulfur at night but still emits it by day." → PASS | FAIL UNVERIFIED_ABSENCE
- notappr: "The drug was not approved by regulators." / "...not approved by regulators until 2019, after a second trial." → PASS | FAIL UNVERIFIED_ABSENCE
Note: in sidefx the `;` split makes the unit "Drug X has no known side effects in mice", and it still passes. The cut-off qualifier is inside the same unit.
Sibling: the same prefix-truncation mechanism already PASSES on base for claims that never classified ABSENCE: "The bridge has never collapsed." / "...never collapsed since its 1995 rebuild, though it fell in 1989." and "The system cannot lose committed data." / "...unless the disk fails." both PASS on HEAD and base (PRE-EXISTING, R27-01b). The root is T1 containment, which ignores a qualifier that comes after the matched span. B1 widens how many claims reach that hole: every negated predicate now does. Systemic fix: when a claim is a strict prefix of its source unit, refuse it if the remainder starts with a restrictor (prepositional/temporal/conditional: in, among, under, at, until, unless, since, except, only, but). Minimum: apply this to negated claims, because a negation's scope is exactly what the remainder changes.
