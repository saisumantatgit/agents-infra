# Research summary: commuter cycling

Participants were recruited from city households. The paper was reviewed by 3 external readers. Results are grouped by age and region.

The paper has 3 authors from 31 institutions. A preprint appeared several months before publication. The journal is published every two months.

They do not claim the link is causal. The authors found a link between commuter cycling and the outcome. Logs without a date were excluded. Participants kept a daily log.

Roughly 59% of participants completed all waves. The sample was made up of 8348 people, about half of them women. The average effect was 3.7 points on a ten-point scale. The planned follow-up wave never took place because funding ended. The authors wrote that the earlier estimate "is not supported by the new data".

The authors report their methods in an appendix. The study used a two-year panel study of 8348 participants. Raw data are available from the corresponding author. Data were collected between 2026 and 2026.

A glossary of terms is provided in the appendix. Figures are included in the supplementary material. The discussion section compares the findings with earlier studies.
