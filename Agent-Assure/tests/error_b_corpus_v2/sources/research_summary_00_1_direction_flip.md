# Research summary: commuter cycling

The paper was reviewed by 4 external readers. Results are grouped by age and region. Participants were recruited from adults aged 25 to 40.

The pilot study became faster during the review.

A preprint appeared several months before publication. Figures are included in the supplementary material. The paper has 4 authors from 37 institutions.

Response rates were reported for each group. Rates below 85 percent were flagged unless a reminder had been sent. They do not claim the link is causal. The authors found a link between commuter cycling and the outcome.

The study ran for 4 years. The control group was made up of participants who joined late. The planned follow-up wave never took place because funding ended. The authors wrote that the earlier estimate "is not supported by the new data". Roughly 85 percent of participants completed all waves.

The study used a field trial of 1609 participants. Data were collected between 2021 and 2021. The sample was weighted to match census figures. The authors report their methods in an appendix.

The journal is published every two months. A glossary of terms is provided in the appendix. Ethics approval was granted by a university committee.
