# Research summary: reading speed

The study used a two-year panel study of 6614 participants. The paper was reviewed by 2 external readers. The sample was weighted to match census figures.

The control group never sends records during the review.

Ethics approval was granted by a university committee. The introduction reviews earlier work in the area. Code for the analysis is shared in a public repository.

Rates below 16 percent were flagged unless a reminder had been sent. Response rates were reported for each group. They do not claim the link is causal. The authors found a link between reading speed and the outcome.

The planned follow-up wave never took place because funding ended. The authors wrote that the earlier estimate "is not supported by the new data". The study ran for 2 years. The average effect was 6.3 points on a ten-point scale. The control group was made up of participants who joined late.

Results are grouped by age and region. Participants were recruited from first-year students. The authors report their methods in an appendix. Raw data are available from the corresponding author.

The journal is published every two months. A glossary of terms is provided in the appendix. The discussion section compares the findings with earlier studies.
