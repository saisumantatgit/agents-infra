# Diagnostic: can a small local checker close the entailment half?

**Status: HHEM COMPLETE. MiniCheck RUNNING** (3GB download in progress).
Written as results land so an interrupted run still delivers what it found.

**The question this was built to answer**, and which the literature cannot:
the research found LLM judges and small checkers both score ~75% on ordinary
grounding benchmarks but collapse to ~56% on FaithBench, which selects cases
where detectors disagree. **Are Agent-Assure's ~31 open classes ordinary-hard
or FaithBench-hard?** If ordinary, buy a checker. If FaithBench-hard,
entailment is not purchasable right now at any price.

Nothing here touches the verdict path. Separate venv, outside `Agent-Assure/`.

## Method

63 pairs, three populations (`build_pairs.py` → `pairs.json`):

| population | n | gold | where from |
|---|---|---|---|
| `attack` | 17 | unsupported | adversarially mined in red-team rounds 7–8, each verified to reproduce |
| `mirror` | 8 | supported | the honest twin of an attack — the source genuinely does assert the claim |
| `gold52` | 38 | Sai's human labels | the entailment-shaped rows of the ratified corpus |

**The mirrors are the instrument's own guard.** A checker that answers
"unsupported" to everything scores 100% on the attacks and is worthless.
Without mirrors this diagnostic could not detect its own most likely failure —
the trap three of this project's instruments already fell into.

**Only 38 of 52 corpus rows are entailment questions at all.** The other 14
(9 absence, 3 unresolved citation, 2 AI-summary) are provenance or policy
questions with no premise to reason over. That is the product split showing up
in the data: **27% of the corpus is not a comprehension problem.**

Thresholds are swept and the checker is scored at its **best** threshold as
well as the default — generous on purpose. A failure at the best threshold is
real, not a tuning artifact.

## Result 1 — Vectara HHEM-2.1-Open (0.1B, CPU, ~4 min for 63 pairs)

| population | n | accuracy @0.5 | accuracy @best |
|---|---|---|---|
| **attack** | 17 | **0.176** | 0.118 |
| mirror | 8 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| gold52 | 38 | 0.421 | **0.868** |

**It catches 3 of our 17 open attacks.** A coin toss would get 8.

On the ordinary corpus it is fine — 0.868, in line with the published ~75–85%
range. On the cases we actually have open, it is far worse than chance.

### What it catches vs what it misses — the pattern is sharp

Caught (score = probability supported; low is correct):

```
0.046  SWAP-1   subject swap: who did what to whom
0.049  NEG-1    "Redis does NOT lose writes"      -> claim "Redis loses writes"
0.298  NEG-2    "found no evidence that margin reached 25%"
```

Missed:

```
0.692  R8A-02   "It is not true that <claim>"          <- SAME MEANING as NEG-1
0.740  R8A-05b  "unable to demonstrate that <claim>"
0.756  R8A-05a  "failed to show that <claim>"
0.760  R8A-05c  "Nobody has established that <claim>"
0.819  R8A-05d  "declined to confirm that <claim>"
0.886  REDHERRING-1  two true facts, causal claim asserted by neither
0.913  R8A-01   "Critics argued that <claim>"
0.938  R8A-03   "A blogger speculated that <claim>"
0.952  REDHERRING-2  the other red herring
```

Three things fall out.

**1. It handles word-level negation and fails on sentence-level negation.**
`Redis does not lose writes` → caught at 0.049. `It is not true that Redis
loses writes` → missed at 0.692. Same proposition, different syntax. This is
the published negation weakness, concrete and in our own data.

**2. Attribution is invisible to it.** Every hedge frame scores 0.74–0.94
supported. `A blogger speculated that X` reads as near-certain support for X.
The model is matching content and ignoring who is committed to it — exactly
what the CommitmentBank literature warned: high scores without learning the
factive distinction.

**3. Red herrings are its worst cases** (0.886, 0.952) — and red herrings are
what STORM's own authors named as *their* dominant failure. The most-missed
class here is the field's known-unsolved class.

### The bias points the wrong way

At the default threshold: **zero** false alarms on the mirrors, **14** missed
attacks. The model errs toward "supported" — which is the Error-B direction,
the unrecoverable one. A checker biased toward saying yes is the worst possible
shape for this gate.

### And a trap we would have walked into

Tuning the threshold for best balanced accuracy moves it to 0.078. That
**improves the ordinary corpus** (0.421 → 0.868) and **makes the attacks
worse** (0.176 → 0.118).

**Calibrating this tier on our 52-row corpus would optimise it away from
catching our actual attacks.** We have a documented habit of tuning against
that corpus; this is the measurement that says not to.

## Result 2 — the number that actually decides it: UNION COVERAGE

Raw accuracy was the wrong question. A 17% checker is still worth having **if
its 17% is disjoint from ours.** So: on the 17 open attacks, what does our
deterministic gate catch, what does HHEM catch, and what does adding HHEM buy?

```
GATE alone      caught 10/17
HHEM alone      caught  3/17
GATE + HHEM     caught 10/17     <-- HHEM adds ZERO
```

**Every single claim HHEM catches, the gate already caught.** The three it
finds — a subject swap and two word-level negations — are precisely the three
our regex rules were built for. The seven the gate misses (`that at least
three…` attribution, `failed to show that`, `unable to demonstrate that`,
`Nobody has established that`, `declined to confirm that`, the nested
`speculated that`) are missed by HHEM too, at 0.74–0.94 confidence that they
ARE supported.

A checker that is 100% redundant with the checker you have is worth nothing,
at any price, in either direction.

### And the finding that answers the proposal exactly

Look at where HHEM *is* right and our gate is wrong — the honest mirrors:

| mirror | our gate | HHEM |
|---|---|---|
| `M-paraphrase` — honest restatement in different words | **UNGROUNDED** (false alarm) | 0.964 supported — **correct** |
| `M-subset` — claim is a subset of what the source says | **UNGROUNDED** (false alarm) | 0.865 supported — **correct** |

**The neural checker's value is exactly inverted from what is safe.**

- In the direction we can allow it (refusing), it adds **nothing** — 0 of 17.
- In the direction we must forbid it (approving), it is **right where we are
  wrong** — it correctly rescues the honest paraphrases our gate wrongly flags,
  which is our entire Error-A problem.

That is the clean answer to "add an LLM pass after the gate to fix
comprehension." The asymmetric-authority design is sound and it is the only
safe design — and under it, a checker of this class contributes zero. To get
value from the model you must give it the authority that destroys the moat.

## Honest limits of this diagnostic

- **The attack set is not a neutral sample.** It was mined to defeat a *regex*
  gate, so it over-represents regex-defeating shapes. It is the right
  population for our decision (it is literally our open-hole list) but it is
  **not** a general claim about HHEM's quality. HHEM's published numbers are on
  ordinary corpora and our gold52 result is consistent with them.
- n=17 attacks is small. 3/17 vs 8/17 expected is a large gap, but the
  confidence interval is wide.
- Both red herrings and several hedge frames come from the same template
  family, so the 17 are not fully independent.
